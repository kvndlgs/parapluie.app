Purpose & context
Kevin is building Parapluie, a comprehensive fraud protection platform designed to protect Quebec seniors from phone and SMS scams. The mission evolved from profit-driven to impact-focused after discovering alarming statistics like a 341% increase in fraud targeting seniors in the Longueuil area. The app features Walter, an AI-powered companion represented as a friendly water droplet mascot who provides real-time threat analysis in French.
The platform targets two user groups: Quebec seniors (primary users) and their adult children (trusted contacts who monitor safety events). Kevin has established partnerships with organizations like CLSC, CAVAC, and FADOQ, and registered "Walter Corp" as his business entity with professional infrastructure including parapluie.app domain and <kevin@parapluie.app> email.
Success is measured by protecting vulnerable seniors from financial fraud while empowering rather than frightening them. Kevin has expressed willingness to transfer control to trustworthy organizations if it would accelerate deployment to help vulnerable populations.
Current state
Kevin has achieved a production-ready platform with both mobile and web components. The Android app successfully intercepts calls and SMS messages, analyzes them for scam patterns using Groq and Llama 3.3 70B, and provides contextual French responses through Walter. He built native Kotlin modules for call screening and SMS interception, integrated with Supabase backend, and implemented Redux state management.
The web dashboard allows trusted contacts (family members) to monitor their senior relatives' safety events in real-time without surveillance-style intrusion. Kevin is currently preparing for Google Play Store submission, having researched requirements for apps with sensitive permissions and Quebec's Law 25 privacy requirements.
Recent technical work includes refining Walter's AI responses (which were initially too verbose) and polishing the landing page with compelling statistics and French-first messaging targeting Quebec seniors.
On the horizon
Google Play Store submission is the immediate next milestone. Kevin has researched the regulatory landscape and submission requirements for apps handling sensitive permissions like call and SMS interception.
Future considerations include scaling beyond the initial Montreal-area focus, potential iOS development for broader Quebec market coverage, and continued partnership development with senior-focused organizations for user acquisition and validation.
Key learnings & principles
Kevin discovered that empowerment-based messaging works better than fear-based approaches typical in security apps. Seniors respond better to educational content and gentle guidance rather than alarming warnings about threats.
French-first development is essential for the Quebec market, though maintaining English documentation supports broader portfolio appeal. Geographic focus on specific Montreal-area neighborhoods with concentrated scam activity proved more effective than broad multi-country strategies.
Technical architecture decisions favor pragmatic solutions over complex implementations - Kevin learned to catch perfectionist tendencies that threatened momentum and make strategic scope decisions to maintain development velocity.

His development approach uses systematic task management with Claude Code for implementation, building features incrementally while maintaining quality standards.
